Booking a holiday for one family is straightforward. Booking one for eight across three generations is different: somebody needs a nap, somebody wants to dive, somebody wants a quiet glass of wine at six, and everyone would rather eat at a different time.

Resorts handle that badly — they spread you across corridors, run meals to the kitchen's timetable, and charge per person per night, including for a five-year-old. The alternative, for a **family holiday in Mozambique**, is to take a whole property instead.

## Key takeaways

- **Take the whole lodge:** two units, **four en-suite bedrooms, sleeps eight**, no other guests.
- **Priced per property, not per head** — the maths improves with every extra person.
- **A self-catering kitchen** means children eat on their own clock; half board and fully inclusive are also available.
- **A high-clearance 4×4 is essential**, and Pomene has no supermarket — stock up in Massinga.
- **Best months with kids:** roughly **May–September** for easy weather; November–March for warm water and turtles.

## Why Pomene suits families better than a resort

Most family-friendly resorts are really family-*tolerant*: a kids' menu added to a building designed around a reception desk. The lodge sits alone on a dune inside the **Pomene National Reserve**, at the end of a sand track — no through-road, no lobby, no other guests walking past your table. For a group with children, that absence is the amenity: you can let them run without watching for traffic or a stranger's sun lounger. Nobody is timing you, and you aren't paying per person for the same beach.

The trade is worth stating plainly: this is remote and off-grid. No shop, no restaurant, no clinic nearby, no kids' club. Families who love it are the ones who wanted that.

## Activities for mixed ages

The activities stack rather than compete — teenagers on the reef while grandparents watch whales from the balcony.

- **Small children:** the sheltered **estuary side** is shallow, calm and bath-warm. Sandcastles on an empty beach; turtle tracks at dawn in season.
- **Ages 6–12:** **snorkelling straight off the beach** (bring properly sized children's masks), **mangrove channels** to paddle, and the **blow holes** at the point.

## Safety, malaria and the drive with children

**Is Mozambique open after the 2026 floods?** Southern and central Mozambique flooded between December 2025 and February 2026. The Inhambane coast has been operating normally again since around mid-2026, and the beaches were never the issue — but some secondary roads and stretches of the **EN1** are still under repair. [Ask us](/#book) for the current route close to your dates, and check your own government's advisory.

The everyday risks matter more:

1. **Malaria.** This is a malaria area. See a travel clinic ahead of time — children's prophylaxis is dosed by weight and some drugs aren't suitable for young kids. Nets are on the beds.
2. **Sun.** Stronger than families expect: rash vests, hats, shade at midday.
3. **The sea.** No lifeguards. Swim on the sheltered side with small children and ask us about tides.
4. **Remoteness.** A proper first-aid kit, spare medication, and insurance covering evacuation.

**The drive:** about **106 km from Vilankulo**, and a **high-clearance 4×4 is essential** for the soft sand at the end. With children, budget half a day, leave early, **arrive in daylight**, and keep water and snacks reachable. No suitable vehicle? Ask about a transfer before booking flights — see our [guide to the drive](/journal/drive-to-pomene-4x4-guide).

## Self-catering with kids

There's **no supermarket in Pomene**. The big shop happens in **Massinga**, the last town on the EN1 before the turnoff.

- **Plan meals before you shop**, then buy to the list.
- **Over-buy staples, under-buy fresh** — leave room for fish bought locally.
- **Bring what you can't improvise:** formula, familiar cereal, nappies, medicine.
- **A cooler box**, packed last — the final leg is slow sand.
- **Water is precious** off-grid.

## Space and privacy for multi-generational groups

What sinks multi-generational trips isn't logistics, it's proximity — everyone in one villa with no door to close. Two separate units solves it: grandparents take one, the family the other, or children go next door within earshot but not underfoot. Because both share one kitchen and one table, the group comes together on purpose — the day scatters, and everyone lands back at dinner.

## When to go

- **May–September:** the easiest window with children — bright, dry, comfortable. **Whales peak in August–September.**
- **October–November:** last whales overlapping the first nesting turtles.
- **December–March:** hottest, warmest water, turtles and hatchlings. The South African festive weeks fill first — there are only four rooms.
- **April:** quiet shoulder, good value, warm sea.
