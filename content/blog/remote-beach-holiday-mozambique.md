The first thing people notice on a **remote beach holiday in Mozambique** isn't the view. It's the silence once the engine stops. No generator, no traffic, no music from a bar down the beach. Just surf, and wind in the dune grass.

Most guides list what's there to do. This one answers the question people actually ask us on WhatsApp: *what will my day be like?* We'll use Pomene, where our lodge sits, because it's about as remote as this coast gets while still having a proper bed at the end of the track.

## What makes a beach holiday "remote" in Mozambique?

A resort holiday is built around convenience. A remote one is built around a place, and you work a bit harder to get there.

Pomene sits inside the Pomene National Reserve, which Mozambique's conservation authority [ANAC](https://anac.gov.mz/en/parques/pomene) lists as established in 1964. It is about 106 km south of Vilankulo, and the last stretch in is deep sand. There's no town and no resort strip. On most days the beach below the dune has more crabs on it than people.

## A typical day on a remote beach holiday

![Infographic of a typical day on a remote beach holiday in Mozambique at Pomene, from sunrise and a beach walk through snorkelling, a self-catered lunch, afternoon exploring and sunset, to a dark night under the stars](/images/blog/remote-beach-holiday-mozambique-day.svg)

**Sunrise.** The lodge faces east over the Indian Ocean, so the sun comes up straight off the water. Most guests are awake for it without meaning to be. Coffee on the balcony and nobody hurrying you anywhere.

**Early walk.** The beach is a 100 m walk down off the dune. Go before the heat does: shells, ghost crabs, the occasional fisherman pulling in a line. In season, tracks across the sand tell you a turtle came up overnight.

**The water.** The reef starts close to shore, and the sea is warm all year. Snorkel, swim, cast a line or, if the wind is up, kitesurf. Divers should book with an operator in advance; our [diving and snorkelling guide](/journal/diving-snorkelling-pomene) covers how.

**A slow lunch.** Back up the dune, into the kitchen. If someone caught something, that's lunch. Nobody is clearing tables at two o'clock.

**The afternoon.** Paddle the mangroves on a rising tide. Walk out to the ruins of the old Hotel de Pomene, a 1963 colonial hotel on the point. Watch the sea spout through the blowholes when there's a swell. Or sleep. That counts.

**Sunset.** Drinks on the deck while the bay changes colour. There is nowhere else you're supposed to be, and that takes a day or two to stop feeling strange.

**Night.** Very little artificial light, so you get the real sky. Between October and March, a red-light walk along the tideline might find a nesting turtle.

## What "off-grid" actually means

Remote doesn't mean roughing it. It does mean a few habits change.

- **Solar power.** Izmaan Lodge runs on solar. Lights, fridges and charging are covered. High-draw things like hair dryers may not be, so ask us before you pack one. Charge in the day.
- **Patchy signal.** You'll catch a bar here and there. Download maps, books and boarding passes before you leave, and warn the office you'll be slow to reply.
- **No shop.** There's no supermarket in Pomene. Whatever you plan to eat comes in with you, so stock up before the final drive.

## What do you eat?

You cook, mostly. That's the point of a **self-catering beach holiday in Mozambique**: breakfast at eleven if you like, fish on the coals when the boat comes in. If you'd rather not, half-board and all-inclusive are available as add-ons, with at least two weeks' notice so supplies can be sourced. ## What wildlife can you see?

**Humpback whales**, roughly June to November, travelling a coastal [migration corridor](https://www.marinemammalhabitat.org/wp-content/uploads/imma-factsheets/WesternIndianOcean/South-East-African-Coastal-Migration-Corridor-WesternIndianOcean.pdf) that runs along this shore. From the dune you look for the blow first. More in our [whale watching guide](/journal/whale-watching-pomene-mozambique).

**Nesting turtles**, mainly loggerheads with the odd leatherback, from October to March, matching the season [recorded in southern Mozambique](https://biblioteca.biofund.org.mz/biblioteca_virtual/celebrating-20-years-of-marine-turtle-tagging-and-monitoring-in-southern-mozambique/). Keep your distance, use red light only, and see our [turtle nesting guide](/journal/turtle-nesting-season-pomene) before you go looking.

**On the reef**: tropical fish, coral and green turtles, year-round.

## Getting there

Most people fly into **Vilankulo (VNX)** and drive south, or come up by road from South Africa through the [Komatipoort border](/journal/komatipoort-border-crossing-mozambique). The last section is soft sand and needs a **high-clearance 4×4** with lowered tyre pressures. Some EN1 stretches are still under repair after the 2025–26 floods, so **check the route before you set off**. Our [4×4 guide](/journal/drive-to-pomene-4x4-guide) explains the sand.

We'll be honest: the drive is the hardest part of the trip. It's also why the beach is empty when you get there.

## Is it comfortable? Is it for you?

![Infographic comparing what you get and what you leave behind on a remote beach holiday in Mozambique, with a packing checklist for an off-grid stay in Pomene](/images/blog/remote-beach-holiday-mozambique-checklist.svg)

You get en-suite rooms, proper beds, a full kitchen, ocean views and a great deal of privacy. You don't get nightlife, shops, steady internet or a tar road to the door.

It suits couples, divers, anglers, photographers, families who like a project, and anyone who wants a week off their phone. It probably isn't for you if you need to stay connected for work, dislike sand driving, or want a buffet and an entertainment programme. Better to know that now.
