If you're planning a Mozambique coast trip for the summer ahead, the timing for **Pomene, Mozambique** is good. Turtle season started this month. The coast has been open since the middle of the year, after the 2025–26 floods. And between now and next September, almost every month gives you a different reason to make the drive.

Here are seven of them, each matched to when you'd actually go.

![Infographic of seven reasons to visit Pomene, Mozambique in 2026–27: the empty beach, reef off the shore, nesting turtles October to March, humpback whales June to November, the estuary, ruins and blowholes, off-grid nights, and a self-catering base on the dune](/images/blog/7-reasons-visit-pomene-mozambique-2026-reasons.svg)

## 1. The beach is still empty most days

From the top of our dune it's a 100 m walk to the sand. On an ordinary morning you'll share the beach with no one, or with a fisherman checking his lines.

That's mostly because of where Pomene is. It sits inside the **Pomene National Reserve**, which Mozambique's conservation authority [ANAC](https://anac.gov.mz/en/parques/pomene) dates to 1964. There's no town, no resort strip and no row of beach bars, and the reserve keeps it that way.

One honest exception. MSC calls at Pomene Bay during its South African cruise season, which [starts on 27 November 2026](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2025/09/msc-armonia-to-debut-in-south-africa-for-2026-27/) and ends when the ship leaves for Europe in early April. On those days the bay gets busy for a few hours. If solitude is the reason you're coming, check MSC's sailing dates against your own.

## 2. You can swim out to the reef

The water stays warm all year, and the reef starts close enough to shore that on a calm day you can walk down the dune with a mask and swim out to it. Deeper reef and drop-offs further offshore are reached by boat. Our [diving and snorkelling guide](/journal/diving-snorkelling-pomene) covers where to go and when the visibility is best.

Fishing is the other half of Pomene's reputation. Reef and game fishing first brought South African families up this coast decades ago, and plenty still come for nothing else. Bring your own tackle, and message us before you travel about boats, because operators here are few.

## 3. Turtle season runs from now until March

From October, loggerhead and leatherback turtles come ashore at night to lay. Hatchlings follow from around late December through March. A dark, undeveloped beach is exactly what they need, and that's what Pomene has.

Watch with a red light only, from behind, and never touch them. Our [turtle nesting guide](/journal/turtle-nesting-season-pomene) explains the rest, including the best weeks for hatchlings.

## 4. Humpback whales from June 2027

If you'd rather wait for winter, the humpbacks pass from roughly June to November. Activity peaks from late July to September, and on calm days you can often see them from the deck. [Our whale-watching guide](/journal/whale-watching-pomene-mozambique) goes month by month.

## 5. It's more than a beach

Behind the beach lies a wide estuary fringed with mangroves. It's best explored by kayak on a rising tide, when the water runs clear. On the point stand the ruins of the old **Pomene Hotel**, a colonial-era resort abandoned during the [civil war years](https://theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/magazine/pomene-hotel-ruins-mozambique-5547348). Nearby, when the swell is up, the sea spouts through the blowholes. ANAC also lists a lighthouse on the reserve's highest point, and flamingos and pelicans among its birds.

It's an easy morning out with a camera. In summer, go early, before the heat.

## 6. Off-grid, and it shows at night

Our lodge runs on solar power, mobile signal is patchy, and there's no reliable Wi-Fi. It can take a day to stop reaching for your phone.

With almost no artificial light for kilometres, the stars come out properly. The evenings are dinner, the sound of the surf, and an early night. Tell work you'll be hard to reach, and download anything you need before you leave.

## 7. A self-catering base on the dune

[Izmaan Lodge](/#lodge) is two timber-and-thatch units with **four en-suite bedrooms** between them, a full kitchen and a deck facing the bay. It's a **self-catering** lodge first. Half-board and all-inclusive are optional add-ons, and they need two weeks' notice, because sourcing food this far out takes planning.

Couples can take one unit. Families and friends can [book the whole lodge for up to eight](/journal/family-holiday-mozambique-pomene). If you're weighing up the meal options, [this comparison](/journal/self-catering-vs-all-inclusive-mozambique) sets out the trade-offs.

## Planning your 2026–27 Pomene trip

![Planning calendar for Pomene, Mozambique from October 2026 to September 2027, showing turtle nesting October to March, hatchlings late December to March, MSC cruise season late November to early April, humpback whales June to September 2027, cooler dry months May to September, and fishing, diving and snorkelling all year](/images/blog/7-reasons-visit-pomene-mozambique-2026-calendar.svg)

**Pick the season first, then the dates.** Turtles and whales don't overlap at their best, so decide which one you're coming for. For more detail, see our [best time to visit Pomene](/journal/best-time-to-visit-pomene) guide.

**Getting there.** Pomene is about 106 km south of Vilankulo. Most visitors either fly into Vilankulo and drive south, or come all the way by road through the [Komatipoort border](/journal/komatipoort-border-crossing-mozambique). Either way, the last stretch is deep sand and needs a **high-clearance 4×4**. Our [4×4 guide](/journal/drive-to-pomene-4x4-guide) explains the sand section. The coast reopened after the floods, but some EN1 stretches are still under repair, so **re-check the route the week before you leave.** We can't yet promise a transfer from Vilankulo. If you're flying in, plan to hire a 4×4.

**Bring it in with you.** There's no supermarket in Pomene. Stock up on groceries, drinks and drinking water before the final drive. Also pack cash, a head torch with a red-light setting, reef-safe sunscreen, snorkel gear and binoculars.

**Malaria.** Inhambane is a malaria area. See a travel clinic before you go, especially if you're travelling with children.

## Who Pomene won't suit

If you want nightlife, shops, restaurants on the beach or a drive without sand, Pomene isn't for you, and we'd rather tell you now. Being this remote is the cost of the quiet. For the right people, that's the point. For everyone else, Tofo or Vilankulo will be easier.
